An IMU reports two things in its own (body) frame: linear acceleration (red arrow) and angular velocity (blue arc). On the figure-eight here, acceleration points toward the inside of each curve and changes direction sharply at the crossing; the angular velocity flips sign as the curvature does. Below, the three signals you'd actually log: forward acceleration ax, lateral acceleration ay, and yaw rate ωz. Pure integration of these would recover the trajectory exactly — which is why the IMU is so useful in short windows. It's also why an integrator drifts: every spike in the trace gets weighted by the timestep twice over.
