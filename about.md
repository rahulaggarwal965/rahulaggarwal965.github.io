Hi I'm Rahul. I currently am a research engineer at NVIDIA working on end to end self driving. More broadly I am interested in embodied intelligence and how robotics can generalizably interact with the human world.

I used to think that robots would need to think as humans do; we are able to "simulate" forward in our own head, and use the results as an implicit plan for the actions we take. I also thought that 3D vision and grounding would be a fundamental part of this equation, being the backbone for how we, as humans, navigate our environments. I thought that our internal 3D structure existed in some sort of implicit latent space, that if we could get robots to exploit, would enable true generalization across the variety of robot-human interaction tasks.

Nowadays, I am not so sure. With how important large scale pretraining and internet data at scale is, it seems to me that the way robotics is moving forward today is through the idea of foundation models — models so powerful that they enable generalization (just as LLMs did) for the variety of daily human life. I don't know what form this foundation model will take (my bet is some combination of video and physics), but I guess we'll see!

Outside of robotics, I really enjoy [reading](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/7235533-the-way-of-kings) and hiking.
