A 2D lidar fires laser rays in every direction and reports the distance to the first thing each one hits. The collection of return points is a scan: a sparse, noisy snapshot of nearby geometry. Drag the robot. In a cluttered room, the scan has corners, doorframes, and obstacles — features the registration can lock onto. In a corridor the scan collapses to two parallel lines that are invariant under translation along the corridor's axis — moving forward looks identical to standing still. In an open field, almost nothing returns within range. The latter two are information-poor, not noisy: a denser lidar would not help.
